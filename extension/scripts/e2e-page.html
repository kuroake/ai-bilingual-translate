The Quiet Economics of Documentation

Good documentation is an economic asset that compounds over time. Every paragraph a team writes saves a future colleague a support ticket, a meeting, or an hour of reverse engineering.

Most teams underestimate this compounding effect. They treat documentation as a tax paid after shipping, rather than as part of the product itself.

Why translation matters

When a project is written in one language, its users are limited by that language. Translation is not decoration; it is the difference between a tool that a billion people can read and one that only a few million can.

  • Documentation reduces the cost of onboarding.
  • Translation extends the reach of documentation.
  • Consistency keeps both trustworthy.
Reading is the cheapest form of travel.

Consider the token budget of a large language model. A sentence with forty words might cost fifty tokens, while the same idea in Chinese could cost thirty. Efficiency is not only about money.
